( The project )

Adult, CBD and other high-risk stores can't just install the usual apps. Mainstream payment providers won't take them, some products can't legally ship to some states, every customer has to be an adult, and most customers want the parcel, the card statement and the emails to give nothing away.

Lilac Hour is a self-initiated concept I designed and built to show how I solve that on WooCommerce. It's a fictional queer-owned boutique with a custom storefront theme. All the commerce logic sits in one plugin, so it can move to a real store unchanged. The products are illustrations and the payment processor is a sandbox.

What I built.

  • 01

    Age verification, twice

    An entry gate on every page, then a date-of-birth check at checkout validated on the server. Only the result is kept on the order, never the date.

  • 02

    Discreet by default

    Plain-box packaging option, a neutral card descriptor shown before payment, and neutral email subjects and sender name for discreet orders. The packing team sees a "Discreet" column in the orders list.

  • 03

    Regional restrictions

    Rules like "toys can't ship to Alabama", set in the admin. They're checked on the cart, live as the customer changes their address at checkout, again on submit, and shown on the product page.

  • 04

    Hosted card gateway with signed webhooks

    The integration pattern high-risk processors use: payment session, hosted payment page, and the order is marked paid only by an HMAC-signed webhook. Replays, stale or forged events and amount mismatches are rejected, and refunds go back through the gateway.
